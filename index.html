    About JobFinder

    JobFinder is a data intelligence platform that centralizes, standardizes, and enriches the fragmented Israeli job market. It continuously cleanses raw operational data across tech, civic tech, public sectors, and healthcare to deliver a high-signal search experience.

    ⚙️ Capabilities

    • Multi-Source Pipeline: Automated ingestion and cross-platform schema normalization.
    • Advanced Taxonomy: Granular classification by role, tier, location, and structural employer type.
    • Embedded BI Analytics: Interactive live-charts mapping hiring velocities, leading companies, and remote-work trends.
    • Salary Reference: External CBS-2023 pay bands (Ministry of Labor / avodata) surfaced per posting and per role — market context, kept strictly apart from the live postings.
    • Productivity Tools: Saved local searches, an integrated application tracker, and CSV data exports.

    📊 Salary reference

    The salary benchmark by role draws on official Israel CBS (2023) reference wages by ISCO occupational domain, published by the Ministry of Labor at avodata.labor.gov.il — a labor-market stock reference, independent of the live postings here. It surfaces two ways: a per-vacancy field-range badge on each job card (a hi-tech-based band for the role, identical across seniority levels — not the posting’s own pay), and, in Analytics, a per-role 2019–23 wage trend and hi-tech pay range.

    🗺️ Location & geography

    Two Analytics charts place each posting’s city on official Israel CBS scales, both 1–10. The socio-economic tier comes from the CBS socio-economic index of local authorities, 2021 (1 = lowest income & education … 10 = highest). The peripherality tier comes from the CBS peripherality index of local authorities, 2020 (1 = most peripheral / farthest from the center … 10 = most central) — a separate axis measuring geographic remoteness, not wealth. Both are external stock references mapped onto our postings; they reflect the sources we track (skewed toward tech and the Tel Aviv metro), not the national job market.
