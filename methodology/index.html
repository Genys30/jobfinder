Methodology

How JobFinder works

Where the postings come from, what each number means, and where the data falls short.

What we collect

JobFinder reads 105 sources every night: applicant-tracking systems that Israeli companies use (Comeet, Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and others), employers' own career sites, universities, hospitals, banks, government and public bodies, nonprofits, and a LinkedIn guest search. A few sites block automated access; those are refreshed by hand about once a week and never by getting around the block.

What a date means

Sources disagree about dates, so each one is labelled by what its date really is. “Posted today” on JobFinder means first seen today. Never compare the age of a posting across sources with different labels.

LabelSourcesThe date is
observed72the day JobFinder first saw the posting
posted10a posting date the source itself publishes
hybrid21the source's date where it gives one, else the day we first saw it
scrape2the day the list was exported, not a posting date (LinkedIn, Shatil)

A date in the future is never shown. When a listing gives an application deadline, it appears as a deadline, not as the posting date.

Open and closed

A role is open while it is on its source's latest list. When it disappears it counts as closed: taken down, which is not the same as filled. A failed scrape never blanks a source: the last good list stays until a new one arrives.

Employers and duplicates

Company names are matched to one employer record, keyed on the Israeli company number (ח.פ.) where we can verify it. The same posting arriving from two sources is counted once on employer pages. Employer pages exist for employers with at least 3 open roles.

Role, seniority and work type

Role group, seniority and remote / hybrid / on-site are read from the job title and posting with fixed rules, not a language model. Titles that fit no rule stay unclassified, so groups never add up to 100%. The rules improve over time, which can move a group's share between months without any change in the market.

Salary figures

The pay range on a posting is a field benchmark: the typical gross monthly pay for that role group from Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics (2023, via the Ministry of Labor). It is not the posting's salary and does not depend on seniority. Technician and trade roles get no benchmark, because the engineering band would mislead. The monthly CBS hi-tech wage in Analytics is an industry average with about a two-month lag.

Signal score

The small “b” number on a posting is the character-level Shannon entropy of its description, in bits per character. Higher means richer, more specific text; low values are usually template ads.

Funding badges

A funding badge marks an employer that announced a round in the last 12 months, from curated press reports. Acquisitions are out of scope. Badges appear only for employers we can match to a company record.

Descriptions

JobFinder shows a short excerpt of each description and links to the original posting. Applications always happen on the employer's own site.

What this is not

It is not the whole Israeli job market. Coverage leans toward tech and the Tel Aviv area, LinkedIn shows only what its guest search returns, and the total grows whenever a source is added. Use the numbers for direction and mix, not as an official count. Official labour statistics come from the Central Bureau of Statistics.
