Policy Implications — where to invest retraining budgets
1

Fund fast-track programs in QA, BI & Data Analytics, and Product

These categories combine high volume, low barriers (37–61% of roles at barrier 1–2) and real junior intake. QA & Automation alone has 126 accessible core openings in this snapshot — the shortest path from retraining to employment for women without a tech background.

2

The experience wall needs returnship subsidies, not more bootcamps

93.3% of roles demand prior experience; only 6.7% are junior. Where years are stated, 45% ask for 5+. Training alone cannot cross this — subsidized returnships and first-job wage support for maternity returners attack the actual bottleneck.

3

Cyber, Algorithms and AI/ML require long-track intervention

The highest-pay tracks are 95%+ closed at entry (barrier 1–2 share: 4.2–8.4%) and feed from the military pipeline (dedicated 8200/Mamram hiring category). Short courses won't open them; funded 1–2 year conversion programs with employer commitments might.

4

The glass ceiling is a stack problem — fund senior-level reskilling

Barrier rises monotonically with seniority (avg 1.79 junior → 3.20 executive); strict-requirement share grows 17×. Women stuck at mid-level need targeted deep-stack upskilling (not generic leadership courses) to clear senior/lead screens.

5

Flexibility mandates matter more than any single program

Only 26 of 4,766 roles (0.5%) are full-remote, and flexibility peaks mid-career then drops at leadership level. Incentives for verified-flexible roles (tax credits per remote/flex position) would expand the market for mothers across every category at once.
